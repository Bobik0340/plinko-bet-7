How the peg pyramid works

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row slightly wider than the one above it. When the Plinko ball lands on the top peg, it can move left or right with equal chance, then repeat that split at every level until it reaches the bottom.

Because every bounce acts like a coin flip, most balls drift toward the center and only a few reach the outer pockets. Mathematicians call this a Galton board, and its path pattern follows a binomial curve. The payout chart simply flips that curve: common middle pockets pay less, while rare edge pockets pay more.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value is usually based on probability, then reduced slightly to include the house margin. When all the pocket payouts are weighted by their odds, the total lands a little below one, and that difference is the operator's edge. In many versions of the Plinko game, the RTP sits around 97% to 99%, depending on the studio and the chosen settings.

A simple truth helps here: rows and risk do not change the long-term math in your favour. They only move variance around, which means they change how bumpy the session feels, not the average result over time.

Licensed versions use a certified random number generator, and provably fair setups let players check the seed values after the round ends. That means the result can be verified, but the ball still cannot be aimed or steered.

Six words worth knowing

Rows
The number of peg lines crossed by the ball; more rows create more pockets and sharper odds.
Risk level
A preset that stretches or compresses the payout table without changing the return much.

The small gap between the true odds and the paid odds that supports the operator.
Provably fair
A method using published hashes so players can verify that the outcome was not changed.
Variance
The amount of movement around the average result, which is what the risk setting controls.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On licensed platforms, yes. The outcome comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you verify afterwards that the result was fixed before the drop.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add more pockets and stretch the odds curve, so the edges become larger but harder to reach. The overall return stays essentially the same.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

None is mathematically better. Low risk smooths the session, while high risk puts more value into rare edge hits. Choose based on your budget and comfort with swings.

Can I practise without spending anything?

Yes. The board on this page uses virtual credits, and many operators also provide a free mode with the same odds. The maths does not change when real money is added.

Does timing or button rhythm influence where a ball lands?

No. The path is set by the random generator, not by how quickly you press drop. Any pattern you think you see is usually coincidence.
